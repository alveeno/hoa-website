100 Points.

An Australian sparkling wine just did what only Champagne had done before.

The House of Arras E.J. Carr Late Disgorged 1998 Magnum has been awarded a perfect score of 100 points by critic Erin Larkin of the Robert Parker Wine Advocate — the most influential rating in the wine world.

What happened

A ceiling that stood for nearly half a century, broken.

For 48 years, the world’s most powerful wine publication had never given a perfect score to a sparkling wine made outside France’s Champagne region. In 2026, that changed — and it changed in Tasmania.

Tasting the House of Arras E.J. Carr Late Disgorged 1998 in magnum, Wine Advocate critic Erin Larkin awarded it the maximum 100 points. It is the first non-Champagne sparkling wine to earn that score in the publication’s history, and the first ever for an Australian sparkling producer. A bottle from a small island at the bottom of the world now stands beside the most celebrated names in France.

If wine isn’t your thing

Picture a perfect 10 — for an entire industry

Imagine a gymnast landing a flawless routine for a perfect 10, or a film that every major critic calls a masterpiece. A 100-point wine score is that moment, captured in a bottle.

The Robert Parker Wine Advocate reviews thousands of wines every year. The overwhelming majority never come close to 100. A perfect score is reserved for wines the critic considers, quite simply, as good as the category can possibly be.
