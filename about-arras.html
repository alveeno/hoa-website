The House

House of Arras

A 30-year quest, at the cool southern edge of the world, to make sparkling wine to rival Champagne.

The story

Tasmania’s answer to Champagne

House of Arras was founded in 1995 on the Australian island of Tasmania, with a single ambition: to make traditional-method sparkling wine that could stand alongside the finest in the world.

Three decades on, that ambition has been emphatically realised. House of Arras is widely regarded as Australia’s benchmark sparkling wine producer, and its flagship E.J. Carr Late Disgorged — first released from the 1998 vintage — is the wine that has now reached the summit of wine criticism.

The place

Why Tasmania makes great bubbles

Great sparkling wine needs grapes grown in a cool climate. Tasmania — far to the south, washed by clean ocean air — is one of the coolest, most pristine wine regions in Australia.

In a cool climate, grapes ripen slowly and hold on to their natural acidity. That bright, zippy freshness is the backbone of fine sparkling wine: it gives the bubbles their tension, elegance and ability to age for decades. It’s the same reason Champagne, in cool northern France, became the world’s benchmark.

  • Chardonnay & Pinot Noir — the two classic sparkling grapes, the same as Champagne.
  • Traditional method — the bubbles form naturally inside each bottle.
  • Time on lees — long ageing builds the toasty, complex character critics adore.
The winemaker

Ed Carr — the “E.J. Carr” on the label

The prestige wine is named after the man who made it: Ed Carr, the British-born chief winemaker who has guided House of Arras from the start. He is one of the pioneering figures of premium Australian sparkling wine, recognised internationally — including honours such as Sparkling Winemaker of the Year.

The E.J. Carr Late Disgorged bottling is his signature: a wine made only in the finest years and aged for an extraordinarily long time before release. The 1998 — the very first of the line — is the bottle that has now been crowned with a perfect score.

The journey

From 1995 to a perfect 100

1998

The grapes that would, decades later, become a perfect wine are harvested — the debut of the prestige label.

2000s–2020s

A benchmark is built

Release after release, House of Arras earns acclaim as Australia’s leading sparkling producer, ageing its wines for many years on lees.

The house marks three decades of pursuing fizz to rival Champagne.

2026

A perfect 100 points

Erin Larkin of the Robert Parker Wine Advocate awards the 1998 magnum 100 points — a world first for non-Champagne sparkling wine.
